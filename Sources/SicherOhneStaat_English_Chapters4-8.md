The demand curve runs from top left to bottom right: The lower the price, the more goods are demanded.

**It's the same in the labor market.** The lower the wage, the more labor is demanded. The higher the wage, the more labor is offered.

### Can Companies Arbitrarily Lower Wages?

They cannot, because then no one would be willing to work. If it were otherwise, there would already be only one-euro jobs today, since companies are always interested in the lowest possible wages. But employees are interested in the highest possible wages, and **they meet where the entrepreneur still makes a decent profit and the employee receives a wage satisfactory to them.**

### Why the State Destroys Jobs

An entrepreneur must, out of self-interest, offer good working conditions because otherwise employees go to the competition. Today there are indeed some companies with bad working conditions. **Every government intervention destroys jobs.** Every regulation increases labor costs so that labor is less demanded. This is a law of nature.

### Globalization and Wage Dumping

Doesn't globalization lead to wage dumping? Indeed, globalization means that certain products are produced more cheaply abroad. **But we all benefit from this.** German workers have more of their wages left to spend domestically.

**Both sides always benefit from free trade**, as economist David Ricardo proved 200 years ago. The Ricardo Effect also works at the individual level. It is precisely the weak who benefit when everyone focuses on what they do best relative to their other abilities.

### The Ricardo Effect Explained

To illustrate the benefits of division of labor, let's go to a small island with two inhabitants, let's call them **Strong** and **Weak**. There are two ways to produce food: growing potatoes and fishing.

- Mr. Strong can do both better. He needs 5 hours for potatoes and 4 hours for fishing.
- Mr. Weak needs 6 hours for potatoes and 10 hours for fishing.

Division of labor now leads to both being able to concentrate on what they do best:
- Strong catches fish
- Weak grows potatoes

If Weak caught fish himself, he would need ten hours. But he can also grow potatoes and trade them for fish. So he only has to work six hours for what he previously needed ten hours for.

**Important: The weaker party benefits more than the stronger.** Strong only saves one hour, while Weak saves four hours.
